# Evidence: ACI ESG and TrustSec SGT attribution

## Decision

The inspected integrations support several distinct kinds of evidence: configured ACI policy, observed ACI endpoint records, ISE SXP binding records, configured Meraki Adaptive Policy groups and mappings, and optional per-flow SGT observations. These sources do not establish one interchangeable or universally current endpoint-to-group relation. Keep the authority, record type, identity scope, and source timestamps with every assertion. In particular, never infer TrustSec SGT from ACI `pcTag`, or treat a configured selector/group mapping as proof of current membership or enforcement.

The earlier conclusion that the DC Networking TA cannot collect `fvESg` was too broad. Its `classInfo` collector accepts configured class names, and the operator guide includes `fvESg`; however, the shipped default does not enable it, and an `fvESg` attributes record alone does not provide all selector relationships or operational membership. APIC 6.2 Endpoint Tracker describes an ESG endpoint as two `fvCEp` objects: one for EPG VLAN binding and one for ESG security classification. The TA has a special `fvCEp` path that retains object attributes/DN and `fvIp` child addresses. This is a feasible collection candidate, pending validation against real, version-specific APIC payloads and parser behavior. The DATA_LAYER `object_kind=unsupported` designation means the graph does not evaluate `fvESg`; it does not mean the TA cannot collect it.

For ISE, `cisco:ise:sgtbindings` is backed by an actual pxGrid SXP binding collector, not just props.conf extraction. When configured, the collector can emit binding records with source and VPN renamed to `src` and `sxpDomain`. The administrative input is disabled by default, and no live collection was verified. Its timestamp checkpoint behavior does not prove complete lifecycle history, removals, expiry, or handling of late-arriving older records. Treat this as observed source-scoped binding evidence until lifecycle semantics are validated. Current membership and effective historical membership therefore remain separately gated.

## Namespace and time semantics

Represent each evidence type separately:

- **ACI configured selector:** fabric/controller, tenant, ESG DN, selector rule, and any explicitly resolved endpoint identity. This is configured intent or a derived selector match, not an operational observation.
- **ACI observed endpoint record:** fabric/controller, `fvCEp` DN, endpoint identity, EPG/ESG classification, and APIC observation time. Preserve both EPG and ESG entries; do not collapse them into one membership without validated correlation rules.
- **ISE configured SGT definition/reference:** ISE authority, numeric SGT, configured name, and observation time from the separate `security_group_tags` collector. This is reference metadata; it does not establish an endpoint binding or session.
- **ISE SXP binding observation:** ISE deployment/server, SGT numeric value, endpoint identity, binding source/domain, and supplied event time. A source-scoped SGT-name lookup is reference metadata, not the binding itself.
- **ISE session/context observation:** ISE authority, endpoint identity, session state, group name, and observation timestamp. Resolve names to numeric SGT only within the same authority and time scope.
- **Meraki configured mapping:** organization, adaptive-policy group/SGT, required IP mapping, and API observation time. Keep configuration distinct from observed endpoint session state.
- **Flow SGT observation:** exporter, flow tuple, source SGT, and flow start/end time. This says what the exporter reported for that flow only.

Keep source event/effective time separate from collection or observation time. A polling snapshot supports “observed at” unless the source and collector semantics prove an interval. Do not invent removal, expiry, or effective-time events. Scope numeric SGTs by their authority (for example, ISE deployment or Meraki organization). SGT and ACI ESG are separate namespaces; ACI `pcTag` is not an SGT. Preserve conflicting claims as separate source-attributed assertions. Endpoint identity must include type and scope: IP-only joins can be ambiguous under reuse, NAT, or DHCP. No selector, mapping, endpoint record, or flow tag alone proves policy enforcement or successful traffic handling.

## Implementation gates

1. Model ACI selector configuration, APIC endpoint observations, ISE SGT definitions, ISE SXP bindings, ISE session context, Meraki mappings, and flow tags as distinct source-attributed evidence types.
2. Before evaluating ACI ESG membership, validate supported APIC versions, exact `fvESg`/selector and `fvCEp` payloads, explicitly collected relationship classes, pagination, duplicate EPG/ESG entries, and lifecycle behavior with read-only real samples. Keep `fvESg` graph evaluation unsupported until its object and relation semantics are implemented.
3. Before deriving current or effective historical ISE bindings, validate SXP payload field meaning, checkpoint behavior, late arrivals, deletions/expiry, and reconciliation against the source. Keep raw observed records distinct from reconstructed state.
4. For ISE session context and Meraki mappings, validate authority scope, group-name or IP resolution, response timestamps, and lifecycle semantics before joining to current membership.
5. Keep NetFlow SGT strictly as per-flow evidence. Use sanitized real samples and source-specific keys; do not invent telemetry or infer membership from names, policy configuration, or flow tags.
