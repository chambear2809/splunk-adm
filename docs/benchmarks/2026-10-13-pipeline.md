# Mixed-source and scheduler evidence — 2026-10-13

The disposable local Splunk 10.6.0.5 lab completed the mixed-source baseline.
The exclusive scheduler rehearsal verified normal scheduling but **failed
automatic downtime recovery**. This is a release qualification gap; resumed
future jobs do not prove that missed data has been recovered. A subsequent
[fresh native validation of commit 4be084b](2026-10-13-native-validation.md)
reproduced the failure: 12 raw flow records, 6 summarized, zero missed-slot
successes, and nine future-slot successes covering all four targets.

[Mixed-source report](2026-10-13-mixed.json) records the tested configuration
fingerprints. The rerun against the corrected shipped defaults and the final

## Scheduler: normal timing passed; downtime recovery failed

`python3 tools/scheduler_pipeline.py --reset-lab`

The real schedules exposed invalid rollup rounding syntax: `@5m` did not
enforce the intended closed window. The shipped bounds now use `-10m@m`
through `-5m@m`, aligned by the five-minute cron. Splunk's parser regression
passed. In the exclusive run, two complete pre-outage cycles followed the
shipped rollup → identity → service binding → service backends order.
The baseline collected exactly six flow records and correctly left the next
batch unprocessed before its own eligible window.

The exclusive run stopped the container at 22:55 UTC, held it stopped for
330 seconds, and requested restart at 23:00:52 UTC. The second batch's eligible
rollup slot was 23:00 UTC. After restart, all four schedules remained enabled
and future scheduled jobs succeeded, but no successful missed-slot jobs were
observed. The summary remained at **6 of 12 expected flow records** until the
30-minute rehearsal deadline. The harness returned a failure and disabled
its schedules during cleanup.

[Exclusive scheduler report](2026-10-13-scheduler-exclusive.json) preserves
the failure. [Read-only diagnostics](2026-10-13-scheduler-diagnostics.json)
record the raw batches, six deduplicated summary records, enabled continuous
schedules, retained SavedSearchHistory records, and effective scheduler limits:
24-hour continuous-search lookback and two-minute history load timeout.
Startup `continued / Could not get next runtime` entries were followed by
future successes, but no missed-slot successes. Follow-up every-minute probes
captured [startup ordering evidence](2026-10-13-scheduler-startup.json): the
scheduler advanced its next runtime while KV Store was starting, then declined
to replace the later runtime with loaded history. A lab-only KV dispatch gate
experiment did not recover missed slots in this standalone environment; no
global scheduler override is shipped. The REST reschedule endpoint also rejected
historical scheduling times. These observations explain the probe behavior,
without qualifying other Splunk versions or deployment topologies. Use the [absolute-slice recovery procedure](../RECOVERY.md) and verify
missing intervals rather than assuming automatic restart catch-up.

## Assisted recovery: retained-outage continuation passed

The initial `--recovery replay --timeout 2400` run established the real baseline,
held the lab down for 330 seconds across the second batch's eligible rollup,
and recovered that slice. It then stopped on a safe empty binding-window warning;
[the first-attempt report](2026-10-13-scheduler-replay-first-attempt.json) remains
available. Follow-up validation exposed the equivalent empty rollup warning and
manual saved-search dispatches appearing in `scheduler.log`.

The harness now accepts only those exact empty-window warnings with zero scanned
and matched events and the expected empty output. It retains the original warning
and an `accepted_empty_window` marker. Other warnings still fail. Scheduled
success evidence requires a `scheduler__` SID; manual dispatches and SID-less
successes cannot certify native recovery or resumed scheduling.

After these fixes, validation continued against the **same retained outage data**,
replaying the original absolute slices, including already written slices. The
[continuation report](2026-10-13-scheduler-replay.json) records all replay jobs and
bounds. It recovered exactly **12 of 12 flow records**, then observed fresh real
scheduled successes from all four targets. Final raw counts, graph totals and
connection counts, baseline dispatch order, and health checks passed. Repeated
identical-slice replay did not inflate deduplicated totals.

This is a successful recovery-and-resumption continuation, **not a clean one-shot
rerun of the final CLI**. The final implementation passed 114 standalone tests
and independent review. The opt-in CI rehearsal now selects assisted replay
explicitly. No automatic production recovery service or global scheduler override
was installed; native restart catch-up remains unqualified.

## Lab isolation and prior findings

A previous attempt was interrupted by another process restarting and
reinstalling the shared lab; its [incomplete report](2026-10-13-scheduler-incomplete.json)
is retained separately. Worktrees do not isolate Docker containers or ports.
All six rehearsal entry points and the lab script now share an ownership lock
across checkouts for the same OS user. CI holds that lock through setup, tests,
and cleanup. Cancellation stops owned subprocesses before releasing the lock.
The scheduler also checks that the container remains stopped during downtime.
Direct Docker commands and older checkouts can still bypass this protection.
