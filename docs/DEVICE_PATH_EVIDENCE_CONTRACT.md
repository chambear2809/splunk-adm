# Device path evidence contract

`resolveDevicePath` is a pure, bounded resolver for separately supplied evidence. It does not collect data, establish source qualification, normalize addresses, or prove that traffic traversed a device. Its output is not an authorization decision. The caller must authenticate sources, construct the complete authority inventory, and supply only records whose identity and snapshot semantics have been independently verified. Exact address strings must already be canonicalized consistently. An empty or incomplete authority inventory cannot establish completeness.

The query specifies a fabric, routing domain, finite as-of time, receipt cutoff, maximum evidence age, and maximum receipt delay, in Unix seconds. Every required authority in that scope must supply exactly one complete snapshot for each of ownership, attachment, and topology. An authority is the exact tuple of source, fabric, routing domain, and layer. Its expected record count must equal the supplied count, including zero for a verified empty capture. Completeness is an upstream attestation: count equality alone does not establish a complete poll. Snapshot ordering, deletion semantics, validity intervals, and omitted records must be established upstream. Multiple captures from the same authority fail closed; the resolver does not guess which is newer. Records have unique IDs per capture, observation and receipt timestamps, and provenance. Duplicate IDs, conflicting replays, invalid records, timestamps outside the windows, and missing or unauthorized snapshots suppress the result. Evidence outside the selected fabric and routing domain is not joined.

Ownership records bind a canonical address to a source-qualified identity. AppDynamics identities and TeaStore OTel identities are distinct namespaces: equality of address, name, or ID never merges them. Every address must have exactly one identity across all qualified ownership captures, and it must match the explicitly selected identity. Attachments independently bind that identity to a device and interface; multiple locations, including evidence of movement, are ambiguous. Neither an AppDynamics address claim nor an OTel identity alone establishes device attachment. The caller must provide qualified ownership and attachment evidence, with unambiguous identity authority and time semantics. A point-in-time inventory claim cannot be promoted to historical ownership without an independently qualified historical contract.

The 2026-10-31 TA-native ACI captures have independently validated two-pass capture integrity, bounded collection times and indexed receipts. Those checks qualify the raw capture envelope, not workload ownership, routing-domain bindings, attachment semantics or historical continuity. ACI path candidates therefore remain quarantined. TeaStore pod watch evidence must meet its own finite epoch contract; it cannot establish Node UID-to-node-IP continuity, and any watch-loss reset breaks the affected continuity. Setting `producer` to `other` is not a bypass: it is an assertion that a different producer contract has actually been independently qualified. NAT, overlay, and unknown translation semantics fail closed. Attachments require interfaces; topology links require both interfaces and an exporter. Each link must explicitly assert no unresolved translation. Missing topology, missing observations, and disconnected devices do not prove that a path does not exist.

The resolver enumerates simple undirected topology paths as **candidates only**, retaining each link's source, snapshot, record, and timestamps. It preserves alternatives, including parallel links, rather than choosing an ECMP route. `traversal` is always `not_observed`; separate qualified packet or flow evidence would be required to support an observation of traversal, and is not accepted by this resolver. A zero-hop candidate means both attachments name the same device, not that traffic crossed it. Input, hop, path and search-expansion caps fail closed; paths are discarded when enumeration is truncated. Failure codes and available provenance expose why attribution was suppressed, but do not assert absence of evidence in the underlying network.
